How contracts lawyers are actually using AI

Eight practical uses for a transactional or fractional general counsel practice. They're grounded in how solo and small-firm lawyers work today, with the guardrails that keep privileged work protected.

01

Drafting and review

This is where contracts lawyers get the most time back. AI does the first pass and the tedious checks. You do the judgment.

Touches client material: use an approved tool, with client consent Public or non-client information only
01Client material

First drafts from an intake form

The client fills out a short form: parties, term, fees, key business points. AI builds a first draft from your templates and standard positions, not a generic form from the internet.

The winYou start from a draft that's mostly there, not a blank page or an old deal you have to scrub.

The clean-up pass

04Client material

A one-page "what this contract means for you" for the client's operators: key dates, who owes what, and what triggers a breach.

The winFewer "quick question about the contract" emails, and a client who sees the value of your work.

03

The guardrails lawyers are using

The ethics rules didn't change for AI. They now apply to new tools. These three habits cover most of it.

Consent before client data goes in

ABA Formal Opinion 512 (2024) calls for informed client consent before representation information goes into an AI tool. It also says boilerplate engagement-letter language isn't enough. Read the opinion.

Every output is a draft

AI does the first pass. You verify it before anything goes out under your name, the same way you'd review a junior associate's work.

Local models for the sensitive work

For privileged material, open models running on your own hardware keep everything on-site. They aren't quite frontier speed, but they're close enough for most drafting and review.

04

Where to start

You don't need a new platform or a "legal AI" subscription. The underlying models are the same. What matters is how you set them up.

  1. Pick one public-information use firstA regulatory watch (#8) proves the workflow with zero privilege risk.
  2. Write your playbook down onceYour standard positions become the instructions for drafting (#1) and review (#2).
  3. Test on a sample contractRun it on a non-client document until the output is consistent every time.
  4. Then bring in client work, the right wayApproved tool, client consent, and your review on every output.

A note on "consistent every time"

The biggest frustration with chat-based AI is that it formats things differently every time. The fix is to give it written, detailed instructions (your templates, your positions, your format) so the output looks the same on every run.

Talk it through out loud if that's easier. Voice-to-text gives AI far better context than a two-line prompt.
